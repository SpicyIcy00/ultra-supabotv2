What this is, and why it looks like this

  1. The unit is a situation, not an answer. George noticed Rockwell at 07:41 and had checked four things and ruled out two by 07:58, before you were involved. What you open is his conclusion with its working shown — what is happening, why he thinks so, what supports it, what he ruled out, what he still can't see, what he'd do.
  2. Confidence is a structure you can read, not a score. Known, likely, possible, unknown are four rows with four different marks. He is allowed to say "possible" and "unknown" out loud; he is not allowed to blur them.
  3. Evidence sits on the step it supports. There is no gallery of charts. Each read appears once, under the question it answered, with its source line. Every figure has a receipt.
  4. His attention is a short, ordered list you can audit — including what he chose not to raise. "Checked · nothing to do" is on screen because trust is built on the things he didn't bother you with.
  5. Steering transforms the situation in place. "Show me the products" and "against OPUS" rearrange what's here; they don't stack a new answer under it. Tap a row and it becomes a reference the composer carries.
  6. Decisions arrive with their authority attached. The one blue frame on the page is the one thing that is yours. Under it, in plain words, is what George may do alone today — and the button that widens it is a sentence, not a setting.
  7. A chart is a sentence he chose to draw. Its shape is picked by what the step asserts — a band for “outside its usual”, a strip of days for “was it on the shelf”, a flow for “where it went”, a chain for “why these are one story”. Every figure is a returned row; the shape and the one-line note are his. See how he draws.
  8. Three parts, by what each is for. On the left, what persists — pages, systems, automations and watches, people — with a pip that says running, off, or needs you; it is a list of things you own, not a menu of screens. In the middle, the situation, at a reading width for his sentences and the full column for evidence. On the right, his mark: it breathes slowly when he is quiet, pulses once per read with the read named under it, turns to writing when he is connecting what the reads establish, and goes warm only when he is waiting on you. It never moves for effect.
  9. Borrowed, and from where. Confidence that opens into what supports, contradicts or is silent (Consensus, Elicit, scite); a visible “no read” mark on any claim without one (Claude, Consensus); his work disclosed in layers — a line, then steps, then each read’s inputs and time (the 2025–26 agent deployments Zylos studied: no mid-run visibility tripled abandonment); one step redone or skipped rather than approve-all (the same); his authority per kind of action as a control you can move, defaulting conservative (Mantlr, Claude’s tool permissions); Later and j/k on the list (Linear triage); a graphite ground with borders as translucent white and colour only where it means something (Linear, Geist); every chart titled by its conclusion with one interpretive note (FT, Datawrapper).
  10. Nothing here is a chat bubble, a KPI grid, a feed or a canvas. It is a document George wrote this morning about your business, with the parts you can push on.
