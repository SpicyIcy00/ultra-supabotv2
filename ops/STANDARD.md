# The standard — what George must be, in the owner's own words

*Moved verbatim out of CLAUDE.md on 2026-09-12 (P0.2) so the rulebook could fit
on one screen. Not one word is edited and none of it is superseded. CLAUDE.md,
ops/NOW.md and ops/DECISIONS.md all point here.*

*Given 2026-09-09, restated verbatim 2026-09-10 with the instruction to
remember it. **This is the standard.** Everything below this section — the
Understand/Build/Run flow, the phases, the experience rules — is a READING of
it, and where a reading and this section disagree, this section wins.*

*It is here because the failure it corrects is specific and has happened: work
gets measured against a phase plan distilled from this document instead of
against the document, and a narrower engineering restatement quietly becomes
the goal. By 2026-09-10 that had produced an excellent renderer for one
conversation turn.*

> George is a generative AI business operating system that I work inside with my
> businesses. It should combine the intelligence of a highly capable AI agent
> with a dynamic interface that changes around what we're doing.

**The real features that matter:**

1. **Generative UI.** George doesn't have one fixed dashboard or always respond
   with text. He can dynamically compose the workspace using widgets, data
   visualizations, tables, comparisons, timelines, documents, controls, business
   objects, recommendations, status objects, etc. The interface changes
   depending on the work.
2. **Transforming workspace.** Follow-ups don't create another answer underneath
   the last one. If I'm looking at Rockwell and say "Products," the existing
   workspace transforms into the relevant product view. "Compare with OPUS"
   brings OPUS into that same workspace. "Why?" reveals the evidence behind what
   we're already looking at.
3. **Interactive widgets and objects.** The UI should be touchable and useful.
   Stores, products, metrics, systems, findings, approvals, processes,
   documents, etc. can become interactive objects/widgets rather than everything
   being prose or standard cards.
4. **Expressive visualizations.** Normal charts when they're best, but also more
   expressive/spatial/tactile visualizations when they communicate something
   better. Different information can have different visual forms instead of
   everything being forced into the same dashboard card.
5. **Ask for anything.** Data, analysis, explanations, comparisons,
   visualizations, operational information, or business questions — without
   navigating to the correct page first.
6. **Contextual conversation.** Short things: "Why?" "These two." "Products."
   "Last month." "No, exclude Air." George understands what I'm referring to
   from the current workspace.
7. **Touch + language together.** Select two stores → "Compare these." Touch a
   product → "Why is this down?" Voice should eventually work the same way.
8. **Deep business understanding.** The connected business, not merely isolated
   database tables. Stores, products, inventory, sales, warehouse, suppliers,
   processes, people, systems, documents become connected context.
9. **Autonomous investigation.** George can decide where to look next instead of
   requiring me to specify every query.
10. **Judgment and prioritization.** "Rockwell is what I'd look at first," "OPUS
    looks fine," "I don't think that's important," "I haven't established the
    cause yet."
11. **Persistent understanding.** George remembers relevant discoveries,
    decisions, unresolved questions, and what he currently understands about the
    business. Every interaction shouldn't start from zero.
12. **Proactive intelligence.** Eventually George notices important changes
    without me asking, investigates them, and surfaces them when they actually
    deserve my attention.
13. **Visible work.** I should be able to feel George working — checking data,
    bringing evidence into the workspace, updating what we're looking at —
    without fake thinking animations or exposing chain-of-thought.
14. **Pages / saved work.** "Save this to a page." "Keep this." George can create
    persistent business pages/workspaces from what we've made.
15. **Build with George.** I can brainstorm a business tool/system and then tell
    him "Build it." Pages, tools, processes, workflows, approval systems,
    purchasing systems, operational interfaces — iterated conversationally.
16. **Generative building.** Building shouldn't require a no-code editor. "Add
    supplier lead time." "Managers can request this but only I can approve it."
    "Show warehouse stock here."
17. **Automations and workflows.** "Run this every Monday." "Check this daily."
    "Prepare purchase orders every week." "Tell me when stock falls below this."
18. **Run what we build.** Not limited to creating workflows — George can operate
    the systems we've created: monitoring, preparing work, checking conditions,
    following up, handling routine work, escalating exceptions.
19. **Approvals / needs attention.** When George genuinely needs me, those
    decisions surface clearly. Approve, reject, change something, or give a new
    standing instruction.
20. **Inbox / attention system.** A place for things that genuinely require me —
    not a notification dump.
21. **Today / awareness.** An immediate sense of what is happening across my
    businesses now — without becoming a static executive dashboard.
22. **Systems that persist.** Things George builds aren't disposable AI outputs.
    They become real parts of the business that continue operating.
23. **Cross-business capability.** George works across all my businesses and
    understands which business/store/system I'm referring to.
24. **Documents and unstructured information.** Emails, documents, supplier
    information, files, messages, procedures.
25. **Actions and integrations.** George interacts with the actual tools the
    businesses use — not merely tells me what I should do in them.
26. **Voice.** Operate George naturally by speaking, especially combined with
    whatever is currently on screen.

**And the process rule that governs all of it:** *a phase ends when the person
using George says it feels right — never when the architecture is finished.*
